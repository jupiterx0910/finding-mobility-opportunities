# Synthetic Example — B2B Sales → Vertical Distribution

> **Fictional operator archetype. Not based on any real user or private conversation.**

## Operator

A fictional industrial B2B salesperson has several years of experience selling standardized maintenance consumables to factories. They understand buyers, objections, reorder cycles and supplier quality, but do not own a product, warehouse or factory.

## Why this career is close to entrepreneurship

The role already accumulates four startup ingredients:

- **payer proximity** — procurement and maintenance budgets are visible;
- **transaction frequency** — many items reorder;
- **demand language** — the salesperson hears why buyers switch or refuse;
- **supplier awareness** — quality, lead-time and service differences are visible.

## Structural Need

Factories repeatedly need consumables and replacement items to avoid downtime.

## Opening Change

The opportunity does not require a new technology. It can appear when fragmented suppliers, digital sourcing, smaller MOQs or better logistics make it possible for a small operator to aggregate demand more efficiently.

## Concrete Offer

Start with a narrow set of high-frequency SKUs where buyers repeatedly complain about lead time, MOQ or inconsistent quality.

**Payer:** procurement / maintenance budget.

**Offer:** managed replenishment + reliable sourcing for a small SKU set.

## 30–60 Day Paid Test

1. Interview 10 qualified buyers about stockouts and reorder pain.
2. Select 3–5 standardized SKUs with clear specs and repeat demand.
3. Qualify at least two suppliers per SKU.
4. Sell before building a broad inventory position.
5. Fulfill five paid orders.
6. Measure lead time, defects, gross margin and reorder interval.

## Recurrence

The thesis strengthens when the same customer reorders because consumption repeats.

## Power Pool

A replaceable broker has weak power. Power improves if the operator accumulates:

- reliable supply;
- recurring accounts;
- supplier scorecards;
- inventory/reorder data;
- private-label SKUs;
- a dense vertical channel.

## Assetization

`personal relationships → recurring accounts → supplier intelligence → reorder data → private catalog → private label / channel / software`

## Kill Criteria

- buyers praise the service but refuse to move real spend;
- margin after logistics/defects cannot fund service quality;
- supplier reliability remains outside the operator's control;
- customer relationships do not survive outside the former employer brand.

## Verdict

**BUY A REAL OPTION** — transaction proximity and payer access justify a small test; inventory scale comes only after reorder evidence.

# Synthetic Example — Industrial Technician → Service → Product

> **Fictional operator archetype. Not based on any real user or private conversation.**

## Operator

A fictional field-service technician works on industrial equipment and repeatedly sees the same downtime, replacement-part and maintenance problems.

## Why this career is close to entrepreneurship

The technician has unusually high **failure-mode proximity**:

- sees what breaks in real conditions;
- knows which failures create expensive downtime;
- understands which parts are hard to source;
- can distinguish urgent pain from cosmetic complaints;
- has credibility with operators and maintenance teams.

## Structural Need

Factories repeatedly pay to keep equipment running safely and reliably.

## Opening Change

A transition window may appear when components become easier to source, diagnostic tools become cheaper, remote monitoring improves, or incumbent service remains slow and expensive.

## Concrete Offer

Start with one narrow recurring failure class.

Possible offers:

- specialist repair;
- annual preventive maintenance;
- retrofit kit;
- replacement component bundle;
- remote diagnostic service.

**Payer:** maintenance / operations / equipment budget.

## Paid Pilot

1. Document the 10 most common expensive failures in one equipment family.
2. Identify which are recurring and standardized enough to solve repeatedly.
3. Sell one paid service/repair before building a product.
4. Track downtime saved, repeat incidents, labor hours and parts cost.
5. Convert repeated work into a checklist and standard kit.

## Recurrence

Maintenance cycles, wear parts and recurring failures can create repeat demand.

## Power Pool

Power may accumulate in:

- diagnostic know-how;
- parts availability;
- standardized retrofit kits;
- installed-base data;
- trusted response time;
- service contracts.

## Assetization

`field knowledge → repair service → SOP → diagnostic database → standard kit → proprietary component / monitoring product`

## Kill Criteria

- every job is unique;
- liability/safety requirements exceed the operator's capabilities;
- customer downtime economics do not justify pricing;
- the solution depends on unauthorized access to employer or OEM proprietary information.

## Verdict

**BUY A REAL OPTION** — service is the cheapest way to learn whether repeated field pain can become a standardized product.

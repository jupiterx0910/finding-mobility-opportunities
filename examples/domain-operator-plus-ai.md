# Synthetic Example — Domain Operator + AI → Vertical AI Service

> **Fictional operator archetype. Not based on any real user or private conversation.**

## Operator

A fictional operations professional knows one recurring industry workflow deeply and has learned to use modern AI tools, automation and agents. They are not trying to build a foundation model.

## Why this career is close to entrepreneurship

The edge is the combination:

```text
domain workflow knowledge
+
real payer language
+
AI-enabled cost compression
```

The operator already knows where people waste time, where errors happen, what must be reviewed by humans and what outcomes buyers care about.

## Structural Need

The underlying business process already exists and already consumes labor or service budget.

## Opening Change

AI can make research, drafting, classification, follow-up, analysis, document handling and workflow coordination cheaper. This may allow a 1–3 person team to deliver work that previously needed a much larger service team.

## Concrete Offer

Do not start with “AI platform.”

Start with one measurable workflow outcome, for example:

- faster quote preparation;
- automated compliance-document preparation;
- customer-support resolution;
- supplier research;
- contract/document review support;
- recurring reporting or analysis.

**Payer:** the department that already pays people, agencies or software to complete the workflow.

## 30–60 Day Paid Pilot

1. Select one workflow with repeated volume.
2. Measure the current baseline: time, labor, error rate, turnaround or cost.
3. Sell a paid managed-service pilot.
4. Use AI internally; do not require the customer to adopt new software first.
5. Measure outcome improvement.
6. Record which steps repeat across customers.

## Recurrence

The strongest cases sit inside a workflow that happens weekly, monthly or per transaction.

## Power Pool

Generic AI prompts are weak.

Power may accumulate in:

- workflow integration;
- domain-specific evaluation data;
- proprietary process data;
- trust and compliance controls;
- repeatable agents/automation;
- embedded distribution inside one vertical.

## Assetization

`domain expertise → AI-enabled service → SOP → agents → evaluation/workflow data → vertical software / data product`

## Kill Criteria

- customers enjoy demos but will not pay;
- every customer requires unrelated custom work;
- generic tools eliminate the cost advantage before proprietary assets form;
- security/compliance requirements make delivery impractical;
- no measurable ROI emerges.

## Verdict

**BUY A REAL OPTION** — use service revenue to test the workflow before productizing.

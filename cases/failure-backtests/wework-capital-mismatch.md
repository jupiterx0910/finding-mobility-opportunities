# Backtest — WeWork / Capital-Model Mismatch

> Analytical reconstruction. Coworking demand was real; the key false positive was treating rapid revenue and membership growth as sufficient evidence that long-duration real-estate commitments could safely support venture-style expansion.

## 1. Contemporaneous Thesis

The thesis was:

`flexible workspace + community + global brand → dense network → recurring membership revenue → platform economics`

The underlying need for flexible workspace was real.

## 2. Capital Heat

**Capital Heat:** C4.

Large private financing enabled rapid geographic expansion and capacity growth. Capital changed the business itself by allowing fixed commitments to grow ahead of fully proven local demand.

## 3. Payment Evidence

Strong.

WeWork generated billions in membership and service revenue and had hundreds of thousands of memberships. This was not a “no customer” failure.

## 4. Retention / Recurrence

Membership revenue was recurring, but customer contracts were comparatively flexible while many real-estate obligations were long duration.

That duration mismatch matters:

`short/flexible customer commitment × long/fixed lease commitment`.

## 5. Industry Formation

Coworking became a durable category. The backtest is therefore not “coworking failed.” It is about financing, fixed-cost structure and expansion discipline.

## 6. Operator Opportunity

A local coworking operator could still build a viable business with:

- conservative leases;
- management agreements;
- specialized communities;
- existing-space arbitrage;
- enterprise managed offices.

The venture-scale global land-grab model should not be copied automatically.

## 7. Power Pool

Landlords, capital providers and locations with scarce demand could capture substantial value. Operator power depended on occupancy, local density, lease terms and brand/distribution.

## 8. Window

**Window:** Expanding → Capital-fueled Crowding → Consolidating.

## 9. Counter-Signals

- fixed lease obligations expanding rapidly;
- discounting to support longer contracts;
- declining average revenue per membership in expansion markets;
- business economics highly sensitive to occupancy;
- capital availability becoming a prerequisite for growth.

## 10. Earliest Kill Signal

If each new market requires large long-term fixed commitments before local recurring demand is proven, expansion should be staged rather than treated as evidence of product-market fit.

## 11. What Was Unknowable

The exact timing of capital-market repricing and later pandemic disruption could not have been known in advance.

## 12. Radar Verdict

**REJECT capital-intensive global replication without location-level proof; WATCH / START asset-light or locally advantaged variants.**

### Lesson

> Real demand and recurring revenue do not rescue a model whose liability duration and fixed-cost commitments outrun demand certainty.

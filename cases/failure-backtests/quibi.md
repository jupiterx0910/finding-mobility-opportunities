# Backtest — Quibi

> Analytical reconstruction of a heavily funded consumer-media launch that demonstrates why capital, celebrity supply and production quality cannot substitute for persistent user behavior.

## 1. Contemporaneous Thesis

The thesis was:

`mobile-first short premium video + Hollywood production + paid subscription → new entertainment format`

The proposition combined a real macro trend — mobile video consumption — with a specific product-format bet.

## 2. Capital Heat

**Capital Heat:** C4.

Quibi launched with unusually strong financing, senior industry talent, major content partners and broad media attention. That reduced execution constraints but raised the risk of mistaking resource abundance for product-market fit.

## 3. Payment Evidence

Weak to mixed relative to the scale of commitment.

The product could acquire trials and subscribers, but the key unknown was whether consumers would repeatedly choose a paid, mobile-only premium format against abundant substitutes.

## 4. Retention / Recurrence

Retention was the decisive weak link. A content service must repeatedly win attention after novelty, launch promotion and free trials end.

## 5. Industry Formation

Mobile video was unquestionably real. The false positive was treating a true macro behavior as validation of Quibi's specific packaging, pricing and viewing context.

## 6. Operator Opportunity

This is a useful warning against copying a category leader's capital-intensive format. A small operator should test the smallest behavioral wedge first:

- repeated viewing;
- willingness to pay;
- creator supply economics;
- format-specific engagement;
- distribution advantages.

## 7. Power Pool

Quibi paid heavily for premium content while competing for attention against platforms with massive existing distribution and creator ecosystems.

## 8. Window

**Window:** Opening hypothesis → rapid validation failure.

## 9. Counter-Signals

- strong substitutes were already free or bundled;
- the format required a new habit rather than merely serving an existing one;
- huge launch resources arrived before retention proof;
- distribution was weaker than incumbent social/video platforms.

## 10. Earliest Kill Signal

If trial acquisition is strong but post-trial repeated viewing and paid conversion are weak, stop increasing content commitment.

## 11. What Was Unknowable

The COVID-19 period changed mobility and viewing behavior in ways that were difficult to forecast. Quibi itself also argued that timing may have contributed to the outcome.

## 12. Radar Verdict

**REJECT large-scale commitment before retention proof; BUY A REAL OPTION only through cheap format tests.**

- Quibi open letter announcing wind-down, October 21, 2020: https://quibi-hq.medium.com/an-open-letter-from-quibi-8af6b415377f

### Lesson

> Capital can buy production quality and awareness. It cannot buy a durable habit.

# Backtest — MoviePass

> Analytical reconstruction using contemporaneous public filings. The demand signal was real; the false positive was confusing paid subscriber growth with sustainable unit economics.

## 1. Contemporaneous Thesis

The thesis was:

`low fixed subscription price → explosive subscriber growth → bargaining power / ancillary revenue → profitable movie-going platform`

By January 2018, MoviePass had reported more than 1.5 million paying subscribers. That was strong payment evidence, but not yet proof that the service could fulfill usage at a sustainable cost.

## 2. Capital Heat

**Capital Heat:** C3–C4.

Helios and Matheson advanced substantial capital to MoviePass and continued financing expansion. Capital was not merely observing demand; it was helping subsidize the operating model.

## 3. Payment Evidence

**Strong first-order payment; weak economic confirmation.**

Customers paid subscription fees. The critical issue was that service costs could exceed subscription revenue. In May 2018 the parent disclosed an average cash deficit of about $21.7 million per month from late September 2017 through April 2018.

That creates a classic divergence:

`Payment exists ≠ contribution economics work`

## 4. Retention / Recurrence

The subscription model was recurring by design, but recurrence was fragile because economics depended on changing usage restrictions and service terms. When the company materially changed the offering to reduce burn, subscriber losses followed.

## 5. Industry Formation

The service had brand awareness, millions of subscribers and strategic ambitions around film financing and distribution. But those adjacent revenue hypotheses did not eliminate the core requirement that the subscription itself survive usage economics.

## 6. Operator Opportunity

A small operator should not have copied the broad subsidy model.

Potentially stronger wedges would have required:

- theater-specific loyalty or CRM;
- local membership products with negotiated inventory;
- software sold to theaters rather than subsidized tickets;
- niche subscription models where marginal usage cost is controlled.

## 7. Power Pool

MoviePass did not control the underlying theater inventory or ticket cost. A platform that pays a third party's retail-like marginal cost while charging users a low fixed fee can grow quickly while remaining structurally weak.

## 8. Window

**Window:** Expanding → Euphoric → Unwinding.

## 9. Counter-Signals

- cash burn rising with usage;
- dependence on external financing;
- service cost above subscription revenue;
- repeated plan restrictions required to improve economics;
- subscriber losses after tightening the service.

## 10. Earliest Kill Signal

If subscriber growth increases the cash deficit rather than improving contribution economics, growth itself becomes a counter-signal.

## 11. What Was Unknowable

It was not obvious ex ante how much bargaining leverage a scaled subscriber base could eventually create with theaters or studios. That optionality justified experimentation, not unlimited subsidy.

## 12. Radar Verdict

**REJECT the subsidized generic model; WATCH / BUY A REAL OPTION only on variants that control marginal fulfillment cost.**

### Lesson

> Paid growth is not enough. If marginal usage worsens cash economics, scale can accelerate failure.

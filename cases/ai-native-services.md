# Partial Backtest — AI-Native Services

> This is deliberately unresolved. The category is still developing, so the goal is to record a falsifiable thesis rather than narrate a winner after the fact.

## Structural need

Businesses already spend heavily on repetitive knowledge work, customer operations, research, documentation, analysis, coordination and workflow execution.

## Opening change

Modern AI models, agents and automation can reduce the labor required for some of these workflows and allow smaller teams to deliver services previously requiring much larger headcount.

## Power Redistribution

The capability being redistributed is not merely “writing text.” It is increasingly:

- research;
- classification;
- document processing;
- multi-step workflow execution;
- tool use;
- customer interaction;
- coordination across systems.

## Naturally positioned people

The strongest early career founders may be **domain operators who also understand AI**, not necessarily foundation-model researchers.

Examples of useful domain proximity:

- industrial operations;
- finance workflows;
- legal/compliance operations;
- customer support;
- procurement;
- logistics;
- field service;
- sales operations.

## Early signals to monitor

- businesses moving agents from demo to production;
- demand for governance, evaluation, security and observability;
- role diffusion from AI research into implementation and operations;
- paid vertical AI services;
- falling human hours per delivered outcome;
- repeatable workflow templates emerging across customers.

## Payment confirmation

Strong confirmation requires buyers paying for measurable workflow outcomes — not AI demos, hackathons or executive curiosity.

## Industry formation

Potential ecosystem layers include:

- model/platform providers;
- agent frameworks;
- connectors/integration;
- evaluation;
- governance/security;
- vertical data;
- implementation services;
- workflow-specific products.

## Power Pool Hypothesis

A generic wrapper may have weak power.

More durable positions may emerge around:

- proprietary workflow/evaluation data;
- deep integration into systems of record;
- customer trust and governance;
- domain-specific distribution;
- accumulated process knowledge;
- outcome-based recurring contracts.

This remains a hypothesis and should be updated as the market develops.

## Mobility Window

`domain expertise → paid AI-enabled service → repeatable workflow → proprietary data/evals → vertical product`

## Kill Criteria

Downgrade the thesis if:

- buyers refuse to pay after pilot curiosity;
- generic platform features absorb the value layer;
- security/compliance costs erase labor savings;
- every implementation remains unrelated custom work;
- customer retention is weak;
- AI cost/performance fails to support attractive contribution margin.

## Unknowns

- which verticals will support durable margins;
- how quickly platform vendors move up the stack;
- where standards and governance consolidate;
- whether service providers can accumulate defensible data or distribution before commoditization.

## Current backtest status

**OPEN / UNRESOLVED.**

The framework should treat AI-native services as a capability-downshift hypothesis requiring current payment, recurrence and Power Pool evidence — not as a guaranteed startup category.

# Framework Comparison — What Opportunity Radar Adds

Opportunity Radar should not be marketed as “the one framework that replaces all others.” That claim is easy to attack and intellectually weak.

A stronger claim is:

> **Most business frameworks are optimized for one analytical layer. Opportunity Radar links multiple layers into one operator-specific ownership decision.**

Its distinctive unit of analysis is:

```text
structural need
→ opening change
→ career-to-founder transition fit
→ early evidence
→ payer
→ industry formation
→ Power Pool
→ operator fit
→ paid pilot
→ recurrence
→ assetization
→ staged commitment
```

## Comparison

| Framework | Primary question | Very useful for | Gap if used alone | Opportunity Radar adds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SWOT | What are strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats? | organizing a situation | descriptive; does not force payer proof, causal timing or test path | evidence chain + payer + pilot + ownership |
| PESTEL | What macro forces matter? | scanning political/economic/social/tech/legal change | macro change may never create an accessible business | power redistribution + career edge + payer + access |
| Porter's Five Forces | How attractive is industry structure? | competitive intensity and bargaining power | industry attractiveness ≠ founder accessibility or timing | founder access + first-sale route + staged commitment |
| TAM / market sizing | How large might demand become? | estimating scale | giant markets can be inaccessible or low-margin | Power Pool + operator fit + unit economics |
| Lean Startup | How can a venture test assumptions quickly? | validated learning and experimentation | can efficiently test a badly chosen opportunity | chooses structural opening before experiment; adds power and ownership after it |
| Business Model Canvas | How does a business create/deliver/capture value? | business-model design | can be completed for a weak opportunity | requires external confirmation and vetoes before commitment |
| Jobs to Be Done | What progress is the customer hiring a product to make? | demand understanding | does not by itself identify timing, founder access or Power Pool | timing + payer architecture + surplus capture |
| Effectuation | What can the entrepreneur do with means at hand under uncertainty? | affordable loss, partnerships, adaptive action | founder action alone does not verify structural market formation | external signal confirmation + Power Pool + career-transition lens |
| Founder-Market Fit | Why is this founder suited to this market? | founder edge | often under-specifies first-sale design, small-business paths and ownership conversion | transition friction + paid pilot + assetization |
| Opportunity Radar | What changed, is it real, why this operator, who pays, who captures power, what is the cheapest test, and can it become owned capital? | end-to-end opportunity selection | intentionally broad; specialist tools are still better for deep subproblems | connects the layers and makes the decision falsifiable |

# Six Distinctions

## 1. External truth and personal opportunity are scored separately

A strong market is not automatically a strong personal opportunity.

```text
95/100 external opportunity
×
30/100 operator access
=
probably not your startup
```

## 2. Career path is treated as an asset map

Instead of asking only “What are your skills?”, Opportunity Radar asks:

- Which payers do you already understand?
- Which transactions do you observe repeatedly?
- Which supplier/channel relationships do you understand?
- Which problems do you see before outsiders?
- Which of these advantages are portable without using employer-confidential assets?

This makes career-to-founder transition a first-class analytical layer.

## 3. It starts before revenue data is obvious

Opportunity discovery often matters before a category has mature financial history. The framework therefore watches leading signals such as role diffusion, supplier formation, standards, user migration and buyer behavior — but never allows these to replace payment evidence.

## 4. It asks where surplus settles

A market can create enormous value while leaving small operators with weak economics. The Power Pool layer asks who controls customer relationships, scarce resources, distribution, standards, data, switching costs or other sources of persistent bargaining power.

## 5. It connects analysis to a paid experiment

The output is not “strategically attractive.” It must become a testable offer:

`named payer → concrete pain → paid pilot → measurable outcome → renewal condition`

## 6. It ends at ownership, not just revenue

The final question is:

> What valuable asset remains when the founder stops selling personal hours?

That forces a transition from employment or self-employment toward compounding ownership.

# Best Use

Use specialist frameworks inside Opportunity Radar when deeper analysis is needed:

- JTBD for customer insight;
- Five Forces for industry structure;
- Lean Startup for experiment design;
- 7 Powers / strategy tools for durable advantage;
- financial modeling for unit economics;
- regulatory analysis for compliance-heavy sectors.

Opportunity Radar is the **orchestration layer** connecting these questions into one commitment decision.

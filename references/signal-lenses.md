# Signal Lenses — Evidence Stack

Use multiple independent signals. No single lens is sufficient.

## Evidence Discipline

For any claim that can change:

- record `as of YYYY-MM-DD`;
- prefer primary or authoritative sources when available;
- triangulate independent sources;
- compare short-term change with 6–12 month persistence where possible;
- deduplicate companies, job titles, products and repeated reports;
- label confidence **High / Medium / Low**;
- mark unverifiable claims as unverified rather than filling gaps.

Classify signals as **Leading**, **Confirming**, or **Counter-signal**.

## 1. Desire Lens

**Question:** Does a persistent human or organizational need survive the technology cycle?

Look for needs that remain even if the current product, platform or technology disappears.

Why it matters: durable needs are harder to fake than fashionable solutions.

## 2. Power Redistribution Lens

**Question:** What capability used to require a large organization but is becoming accessible to a small team?

Historical patterns:

- internet → publishing;
- ecommerce → retail distribution;
- cloud → infrastructure;
- creator platforms → media production;
- AI → cognitive production;
- agents → execution and coordination.

Ask:

- what means of production has been redistributed?
- what old bottleneck became abundant?
- what new complement became scarce?
- where is the temporary entry window before incumbents rebuild moats?

## 3. Customer Pull / Payment Lens

Always separate:

`User / Beneficiary / Buyer / Payer`

Strong evidence:

- recurring pain;
- explicit budget;
- paid pilots;
- retention / renewal;
- measurable ROI;
- replacement of existing spend;
- organic referrals.

Payment evidence outranks search volume, media attention, downloads, funding or social discussion.

## 4. Talent Flow Lens

Treat recruiting as a corporate budget signal, not proof of an industry.

Track:

- new job titles;
- number of distinct hiring firms;
- salary premium;
- persistence;
- geographic diffusion;
- role diffusion.

Typical role diffusion:

`research/algorithm → product → engineering/test → supply chain/quality → sales/channel → service/operations → finance/legal`

When hiring spreads beyond R&D into product, quality, supply chain, sales, service and compliance, the category may be moving from technology to business.

## 5. Capital Flow Lens

Track:

- financing count and size;
- follow-on rounds;
- failed/down rounds;
- strategic investment;
- M&A;
- capex.

Capital confirms organizational formation and attention. It does not prove customer value.

## 6. Product / Supply-Chain Formation Lens

Look for:

- components;
- ODM/OEM capacity;
- certification/testing;
- distributors;
- aftermarket and service;
- falling component costs;
- faster prototype-to-production cycles.

Use the **Lego Test**:

> Are the building blocks becoming mature while the winning product definition, service layer, channel or demand aggregation is still unsettled?

That is often where small operators can enter.

## 7. Formalization Lens

Track:

`definition → standard → registration → certification → liability → insurance → payment → settlement`

Ask:

- is policy creating a category, enabling a category or protecting incumbents?
- does formalization create new compliance, certification, data, distribution, training, service or financing needs?
- could new regulation erase margin rather than protect it?

Regulatory latency is not automatically a moat.

## 8. IPO / Capitalization Lens

IPO data is a later-stage capitalization-path detector, not an early operator signal.

Stages:

`S0 idea → S1 product → S2 talent build → S3 payer/revenue → S4 private scale → S5 IPO pipeline → S6 public validation`

For many career founders, the highest mobility window is often S1–S3.

Reverse-engineer:

> If this category creates a credible IPO cluster in several years, what suppliers, services, channels, managers, standards, data layers and niche operators must exist before that?

## 9. Public-Market Quality Gate

Check:

- revenue quality vs GMV;
- returns / refunds;
- CAC and paid-traffic dependence;
- retention / repeat purchase;
- gross and contribution margin;
- marketing-to-revenue ratio;
- pricing power;
- customer/supplier/platform concentration;
- cash conversion;
- compliance liabilities;
- what revenue remains if paid traffic stops.

A traffic renter is not the same as a customer owner.

## 10. Mobility Lens

Ask:

- can the opportunity be entered with tolerable balance-sheet risk?
- can a paid commercial test happen within 6–12 months?
- is the operator's existing skill being repriced upward?
- can output scale faster than headcount?
- is there a path from service to owned assets?
- can the operator gain access to the Power Pool?

A 95/100 industry can still be a 30/100 personal opportunity.

## 11. Career-to-Founder Transition Lens

Ask:

- which occupations sit closest to payers and repeated transactions?
- which roles observe recurring pain that could be sold independently?
- which careers accumulate portable supplier/channel knowledge?
- can the first offer be delivered before heavy fixed investment?
- can recurring service become product, data, distribution, software or brand?

Read `career-to-founder-transition.md` for the full lens.

## 12. Youth Migration Lens

Use for consumer, platform, culture, entertainment, education, AI-hardware and youth-led categories.

Track migration across:

1. **time / attention**;
2. **identity / status**;
3. **creation / work**;
4. **money**.

Stronger pattern:

`attention migration → repeated behavior → cross-community diffusion → payment / merchant response`

Do not infer a market from hashtags, downloads or one platform's trend list alone.

## 13. Campus Frontier Lens

Treat campuses as dense experimentation networks with fast peer-to-peer diffusion but limited purchasing power.

Observe:

- tools and apps students repeatedly use;
- clubs, competitions, hackathons and maker activity;
- side hustles, freelancing and resale;
- new content/social formats;
- internship choices and new job aspirations;
- hardware/software combinations students adopt early.

Stronger pattern:

`one-campus adoption → multi-campus replication → repeated use → payer emergence → post-campus persistence`

Campus popularity is an early signal, not commercial proof.

## Counter-Signals

Actively search for:

- attention spikes without retention;
- funding without payment;
- hiring concentrated only in R&D with no role diffusion;
- youth/campus excitement with no payer;
- GMV inflated by returns, subsidies or paid traffic;
- supply expansion faster than demand;
- regulation shifting economics to another participant;
- a Power Pool the operator cannot access;
- a career edge that depends on employer-confidential assets.

# Opportunity Window & Why-Not-Yet Test

A good opportunity is not enough. Timing matters twice:

1. **Why now?** — what opened the window?
2. **How long is the window likely to remain unusually attractive?**

Opportunity Radar therefore adds two checks:

```text
Why-Not-Yet Test
+
Opportunity Window / Half-Life
```

---

# 1. Why-Not-Yet Test

After finding an apparently attractive opportunity, ask:

> **If this is so good, why has it not already been fully exploited?**

This question is designed to kill AI-generated “obvious opportunity” stories.

Possible answers fall into two groups.

## Healthy reasons the opportunity may still be open

- a key technology only recently became cheap enough;
- distribution or payment rails only recently appeared;
- regulation only recently allowed the activity;
- buyer behavior is changing faster than incumbent processes;
- a new bottleneck has appeared that incumbents are not designed to solve;
- the opportunity is fragmented by geography, workflow or niche knowledge;
- the addressable market is too small for large incumbents but large enough for a focused small business;
- required capabilities previously lived in separate professions and can now be combined by a small team.

## Dangerous reasons nobody has solved it

- customers complain but will not pay;
- sales cycles are longer than the economics support;
- implementation cost exceeds customer value;
- the “pain” occurs too rarely for recurrence;
- the market is easy to enter and therefore margins collapse quickly;
- liability, regulation or externalities erase attractive economics;
- the real Power Pool belongs to a platform, distributor, regulator or scarce supplier;
- the required data, customer access or license is not ethically/legal portable;
- incumbents already bundle the solution at near-zero incremental price;
- the problem sounds large but has no budget owner.

## Required output

For every major candidate, write:

```text
Why has this not already been solved?
Best benign explanation:
Best hostile explanation:
Evidence that distinguishes them:
```

A strong analysis should actively search for the hostile explanation.

# 2. Opportunity Window

Do not confuse a structurally good business with a temporarily open entry window.

Use this lifecycle:

```text
Invisible / Impossible
        ↓
Opening
        ↓
Expanding
        ↓
Crowding
        ↓
Consolidating
        ↓
Closed to generalists
```

## Opening

Characteristics:

- capability has recently become possible or cheap;
- payer problem is visible but solutions are immature;
- few specialized competitors;
- customers tolerate service-heavy delivery;
- domain access matters more than scale.

Typical action: **BUY A REAL OPTION / START a narrow paid pilot**.

## Expanding

Characteristics:

- repeatable demand appears;
- specialized suppliers and roles form;
- multiple geographies or customer groups adopt;
- successful wedges begin to standardize.

Typical action: **START / standardize / build assets**.

## Crowding

Characteristics:

- many entrants copy the same offer;
- CAC rises or price competition intensifies;
- platforms and incumbents bundle common features;
- generic service becomes interchangeable.

Typical action: **move deeper into Power, vertical specialization or owned distribution**.

## Consolidating

Characteristics:

- scale, standards, regulation, data or capital become dominant;
- weaker entrants disappear;
- customers prefer established vendors;
- platform economics become clearer.

Typical action: **avoid generic entry; target narrow neglected bottlenecks or adjacent infrastructure**.

## Closed to generalists

Characteristics:

- no meaningful edge for a new undifferentiated entrant;
- margins are competed away;
- distribution and standards are controlled;
- switching costs favor incumbents.

Typical action: **REJECT core entry unless operator has a rare proprietary edge**.

# 3. Opportunity Half-Life

“Half-life” is a metaphor for how quickly an unusual entry advantage is being competed or absorbed away.

Do not manufacture an exact number when evidence does not support one.

When useful, estimate a range and state assumptions:

```text
Estimated window: 12–24 months
Confidence: Low / Medium / High
Main closing mechanism: incumbent bundling
```

Signals that shorten the window:

- rapid competitor entry;
- declining tool/technology scarcity;
- platform bundling;
- supplier commoditization;
- rising paid-acquisition costs;
- standards or licenses that favor scale;
- customers standardizing around a few vendors;
- easy copying with little proprietary learning.

Signals that lengthen the window:

- fragmented local delivery;
- deep domain/tacit knowledge;
- regulation that requires specialized execution;
- hard-to-copy trust/network effects;
- proprietary data accumulated through service;
- high switching cost after workflow integration;
- neglected small niches unattractive to large vendors.

---

# 4. Window-Adjusted Action

| Opportunity quality | Window | Default response |
|---|---|---|
| High | Opening | **START / BUY A REAL OPTION quickly** |
| High | Expanding | **START and assetize** |
| High | Crowding | **enter only with a sharper edge / Power path** |
| High | Consolidating | **target picks-and-shovels or niche infrastructure** |
| Medium | Opening | **small paid experiment** |
| Low | Any | **REJECT / redesign** |

Urgency does not make a bad opportunity good.

---

# Core Rule

> **A window is valuable only when you can reach it before it closes — and when the position you build survives after the window closes.**

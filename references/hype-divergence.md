# Hype Divergence — When Capital and Customer Reality Disagree

## Purpose

Opportunity Radar treats VC / angel / strategic funding as an **environment variable**, not an opportunity score.

The Hype Divergence test looks for disagreement between:

`Capital Heat × Payment × Retention × Contribution Economics × Competition × Power Pool`

The goal is to identify both false positives and overlooked opportunities.

## Four Primary States

### A. Category Formation

`Capital Heat High + Payment Strong + Retention Strong`

Interpretation: the category is becoming economically real.

Do not automatically START. High capital heat may also mean rapidly rising competition and closing entry windows.

### B. Hype Divergence

`Capital Heat High + Payment Weak + Retention Weak/Unknown`

Interpretation: investor belief and narrative are running ahead of customer proof.

Default: **WATCH / REJECT**, or buy only the cheapest evidence-producing option.

### C. Underfollowed Opportunity

`Capital Heat Low/Moderate + Payment Strong + Retention Strong`

Interpretation: customers are voting with money while professional capital remains relatively uninterested.

This can be attractive for career founders, niche B2B businesses and small operators whose target does not need venture-scale outcomes.

Default: **BUY A REAL OPTION / START** only if operator fit, Power Pool and economics also pass.

### D. Unwinding

`Capital Heat Falling + Payment Weak + Retention Weak`

Interpretation: a narrative and financing cycle is reversing without customer economics underneath it.

Default: **REJECT** until a new structural change resets the thesis.

## Subsidy Test

Payment can be real and still misleading.

Ask:

- who ultimately funds the transaction?
- is the customer paying full economic cost?
- does each additional transaction improve or worsen contribution margin?
- is the company using new capital to subsidize existing usage?
- would demand persist at a price that covers variable and replacement costs?

MoviePass is a canonical example of:

`Strong payment + strong usage + worsening cash economics`.

## Retention Test

A first purchase is evidence of curiosity or need. Recurrence is evidence that the solution keeps winning.

Measure when available:

- repeat purchase;
- renewal;
- cohort retention;
- order frequency;
- budget renewal;
- expansion revenue;
- organic referral;
- churn after discounts/free trials end.

Never let acquisition growth hide cohort decay.

## Competition Reflexivity

Capital can make an opportunity **less attractive** for a small entrant.

More funding can cause:

- hiring-cost inflation;
- paid-media inflation;
- supplier scarcity;
- aggressive subsidy;
- faster incumbent response;
- commoditization;
- faster window closure.

Therefore:

`Capital Heat ↑` can increase industry confidence while decreasing operator opportunity.

## Contrarian Opening

Capital retreat is not always a negative signal.

If:

- customers still pay;
- retention survives;
- competitors disappear;
- acquisition costs fall;
- assets/talent become cheaper;
- the underlying need remains;

then `C5 Unwinding` may create a **post-hype entry window**.

This is not contrarianism for its own sake. Payment and recurrence must remain.

## Required Output

For hype-sensitive categories report:

- Capital Heat: C0–C5
- Capital Trend: Rising / Flat / Falling
- Payment Strength: Weak / Mixed / Strong
- Retention Strength: Weak / Mixed / Strong / Unknown
- Contribution Economics: Improving / Flat / Worsening / Unknown
- Competition Effect: Opening / Neutral / Closing
- Power Pool Access: Weak / Mixed / Strong
- Divergence State: Formation / Hype Divergence / Underfollowed / Unwinding / Mixed
- Operator Implication
- Cheapest falsification test

## Hard Guardrail

A category must **not** receive START when:

`Capital Heat = C4 Euphoric`

and both payment and retention are weak or materially unverified.

The correct output is **WATCH**, **REJECT**, or **BUY A REAL OPTION** sized to resolve the missing customer evidence.

## One-Line Rule

> Follow money twice: first the investors' money, then the customers' money. Trust the second more.

# Recommended Reading — Three Books, Three Failure Modes

Opportunity Radar intentionally recommends **only three** entrepreneurship books in its core path.

Why only three? Because a long reading list easily becomes intellectual procrastination. These three cover three different places where founders repeatedly make expensive mistakes:

```text
False demand
    ↓
Bad experimentation
    ↓
Weak value capture
```

That maps to:

```text
Payer truth
→ paid validation / iteration
→ Power / durable ownership
```

---

# 1. The Mom Test — Rob Fitzpatrick

Official site: https://www.momtestbook.com/

## What it teaches

How to talk to potential customers without accidentally collecting compliments, hypotheticals and biased feedback.

The practical lesson is simple: ask about **specific past behavior, existing problems, current spending and real commitments**, not whether someone “likes your idea.”

## Why Opportunity Radar needs it

Opportunity Radar says:

`attention ≠ payment`

The Mom Test provides practical interview discipline for proving that distinction.

It strengthens:

- payer architecture;
- problem validation;
- first-ten-customer interviews;
- commitment evidence;
- Kill Criteria.

## The mistake it prevents

> “Twenty people said they would use it, so demand is validated.”

Friendly opinions are cheap. Past behavior, money, time, introductions and concrete commitments are stronger evidence.

## What it does not cover

It does not tell you which market is structurally emerging, where industry power will settle or how to build a durable advantage. It is a **truth-extraction tool**, not a complete strategy framework.

---

# 2. The Lean Startup — Eric Ries

Official site: https://theleanstartup.com/book

## What it teaches

How to reduce entrepreneurial uncertainty through short build-measure-learn loops, minimum viable products, validated learning and pivots.

Its deeper contribution is:

> **Treat the startup as a sequence of testable assumptions rather than a plan that deserves obedience.**

## Why Opportunity Radar needs it

Opportunity Radar uses:

- 30–60 day paid pilots;
- Leading Indicators;
- Confirmation Indicators;
- Kill Criteria;
- Real Options.

The Lean Startup provides the operating discipline behind that behavior: learn quickly enough that being wrong is survivable.

It strengthens:

`analysis → experiment → evidence → decision`

## The mistake it prevents

> Spending a year building the complete product before discovering that the important assumption was wrong.

## What it does not cover

Lean experimentation can efficiently test a **badly chosen opportunity**. It does not by itself answer:

- Why now?
- Why this career/operator?
- Where is the Power Pool?
- Can the result become owned capital?

Opportunity Radar therefore starts before Lean Startup and continues after it.

---

# 3. 7 Powers — Hamilton Helmer

Official site: https://7powers.com/

## What it teaches

A first-principles way to think about the conditions that allow a business to earn persistent differential returns rather than being competed back toward average economics.

The book focuses on **Power**: structural conditions that let a company sustain an advantage while creating value.

## Why Opportunity Radar needs it

Many founders successfully find demand and still build weak businesses.

They have customers, revenue and growth, but no durable control point.

Opportunity Radar's **Power Pool** and **assetization** questions need exactly this strategic layer.

It strengthens:

`recurrence → bargaining power → economic rent → owned capital`

## The mistake it prevents

> Confusing “people buy from us today” with “we will retain attractive economics after competitors arrive.”

## What it does not cover

7 Powers is not primarily a customer-discovery or early-pilot manual. A founder can understand Power intellectually and still fail to get the first customer.

That is why it is third, not first.

---

# Why These Three?

Because they form a clean progression:

## Book 1 — Find the truth

**The Mom Test**

> Is the problem/payment real, or are people being polite?

## Book 2 — Learn without dying

**The Lean Startup**

> What is the cheapest experiment that can make us less wrong?

## Book 3 — Build something worth owning

**7 Powers**

> If it works, why won't competition take the economics away?

Together:

```text
Customer Truth
      ↓
Validated Learning
      ↓
Durable Power
```

Or in Opportunity Radar language:

```text
Payer → Pilot → Recurrence → Power Pool → Owned Capital
```

Reading order:

1. **The Mom Test** — read before customer interviews.
2. **The Lean Startup** — use while running paid pilots.
3. **7 Powers** — use once demand is real and you must decide what durable advantage to build.

Reading is not validation. After each book, the correct next step is still the market.

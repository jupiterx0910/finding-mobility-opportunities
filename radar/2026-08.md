# 1. Enterprise Agent Production Deployment, Governance & Evaluation

## Observable facts

OpenAI's July 2026 **Presence** product is explicitly positioned around deploying governed agents into high-volume enterprise workflows, with policies, permissions, system connections, testing, monitoring and human escalation. OpenAI Frontier also emphasizes enterprise context, production execution, evaluation loops, controls and auditing.

Microsoft's May 2026 agent-governance guidance focuses on governing agents at scale and highlights security, observability, discoverability, sharing controls and lifecycle administration.

Primary sources:

- OpenAI Presence: https://openai.com/index/introducing-openai-presence/
- OpenAI Frontier: https://openai.com/business/frontier/
- Microsoft agent governance update: https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/copilot-studio-blog/new-updates-administering-and-governing-agents-whitepaper-v3-2/4517044

## Opportunity Radar inference

The bottleneck may be shifting from **“can we build an agent demo?”** to **“can this agent operate safely, measurably and reliably inside a real workflow?”**

That creates potential small-team wedges around:

- vertical agent implementation;
- workflow evaluation and regression testing;
- permissions/governance setup;
- production observability;
- migration from fragile pilots to monitored workflows;
- human-escalation and exception design.

## Payer hypothesis

- CIO / IT transformation budget;
- business-function owner;
- security/governance budget;
- customer-operations budget.

## Best-fit career transitions

- enterprise software/IT integrator + AI;
- regulated-domain operator + AI;
- security/compliance operator + AI;
- customer-operations leader who understands the workflow and payer.

## Power Pool hypothesis

Generic prompt-building is likely weak. Stronger power may form around:

- deep workflow integration;
- proprietary evaluation data;
- domain-specific guardrails;
- trusted deployment history;
- accumulated business context and outcome data.

## Leading indicators

- more enterprises move from pilots to production;
- new roles around agent governance/evaluation/operations;
- demand for monitored, auditable agent workflows;
- repeat implementation patterns by vertical.

## Confirmation indicators

- paid recurring deployment/governance contracts;
- measurable labor/cycle-time improvement;
- renewal/expansion;
- falling implementation hours per customer.

## Kill criteria

- platform vendors absorb the entire implementation/governance layer at near-zero incremental cost;
- enterprises remain stuck at experimentation with little recurring spend;
- service economics remain fully custom and labor-linear.

## Current stance

**BUY A REAL OPTION** — sell one narrow production workflow before building a broad platform.

# 2. Lightweight AI Application Services for Manufacturing SMEs

## Observable facts

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology published the **2026 “small, fast, lightweight, precise” digital product/service guidance** in July 2026, emphasizing better-fit digital services for SMEs.

A March/April 2026 MIIT action on inclusive computing explicitly encourages cultivating AI application service providers and promoting low-cost, lightweight, easy-to-deploy solutions for manufacturing SMEs across R&D, production, equipment maintenance and supply-chain management.

The 2026 “model-data resonance” action also calls for industry models, specialized agents, high-quality datasets and application scenarios across many industrial sectors.

Primary sources:

- MIIT “小快轻准” 2026 guidance: https://wap.miit.gov.cn/jgsj/qyj/wjfb/art/2026/art_2ce9b878c2c34be4b8e0f9b275b22fbd.html
- MIIT inclusive-computing SME action: https://www.miit.gov.cn/zwgk/zcwj/wjfb/tz/art/2026/art_58259bfb30924d6bb225b82b66d1008d.html
- MIIT / National Data Administration “模数共振” 2026: https://www.miit.gov.cn/jgsj/kjs/wjfb/art/2026/art_71c830bf78a94f498bd3c187ac8b4778.html

## Opportunity Radar inference

The accessible opportunity may not be “build another general AI platform.” It may be:

> **industry operator + AI + one measurable SME workflow**

Potential wedges:

- equipment-maintenance analysis;
- quality/document workflows;
- supplier research and procurement operations;
- quoting/technical-document preparation;
- production/operations reporting;
- sales/service workflow automation.

## Payer hypothesis

- factory owner / GM;
- operations budget;
- digital-transformation budget;
- department budget replacing manual service/labor.

## Best-fit career transitions

- industrial software/automation engineer;
- manufacturing domain operator + AI;
- industrial service technician + AI;
- procurement/supply-chain operator + AI.

## Power Pool hypothesis

Power is more likely in:

- deep vertical workflow knowledge;
- proprietary process/evaluation data;
- integration into existing factory systems;
- trusted local delivery;
- repeatable “small-fast-light-precise” packages.

## Leading indicators

- more industry-specific agents and datasets;
- SME procurement of outcome-oriented AI services;
- local service-provider ecosystems expanding;
- repeated deployments in the same sub-industry.

## Confirmation indicators

- paid projects with measurable ROI;
- renewals/expansion;
- standard packages replacing bespoke consulting;
- founder-hours per deployment falling.

## Kill criteria

- buyers require heavy custom integration for every project;
- ROI cannot be measured;
- generic vendor features erase the service layer;
- SME willingness to pay remains low despite policy support.

## Current stance

**BUY A REAL OPTION** — start with a paid workflow service, not software-first.

# 3. Embodied-AI Field Deployment, Training Data & Integration

## Observable facts

In June 2026, MIIT and the State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission launched a joint program for **real-world training of humanoid robots and embodied AI**. The program emphasizes real production/service environments, application consortia, high-quality real-machine data, deployment validation, safety, lifecycle support and scalable application.

The notice specifically describes roles for user organizations, whole-machine companies or **application service providers**, supply-chain companies and research organizations. It also encourages “robot as a service” models and deployment/operation support.

Primary source:

- MIIT + SASAC 2026 real-world training action: https://www.miit.gov.cn/zwgk/zcwj/wjfb/tz/art/2026/art_f291ccd3da4c47ce95741de63cc088e6.html

## Opportunity Radar inference

A career founder does not need to manufacture a humanoid robot to participate.

Accessible wedges may appear around:

- site adaptation and deployment;
- teleoperation / data collection operations;
- task-skill data pipelines;
- validation and testing;
- safety/maintenance operations;
- integration with factory workflows;
- robot-as-a-service field operations.

## Payer hypothesis

- industrial user unit;
- robot manufacturer/integrator;
- application-validation budget;
- deployment/operations budget.

## Best-fit career transitions

- industrial automation/robotics integrator;
- field-service technician;
- manufacturing process engineer;
- simulation/data operator;
- safety/validation specialist.

## Power Pool hypothesis

Weak position: generic hardware resale.

Potential stronger positions:

- hard-to-replicate field data;
- high-value scenario know-how;
- deployment reliability history;
- integration into real production processes;
- maintenance/safety service network.

## Leading indicators

- more real sites open for training and validation;
- more task-specific deployment contracts;
- application service providers appear by vertical;
- standards/testing requirements become clearer.

## Confirmation indicators

- repeated commercial deployments;
- measured labor/throughput/safety ROI;
- paid multi-site rollout;
- recurring operation/maintenance revenue.

## Kill criteria

- economics remain demo-driven rather than production-driven;
- hardware reliability prevents repeat use;
- deployment requires capital beyond the operator's balance sheet;
- manufacturers internalize all integration/service economics.

## Current stance

**BUY A REAL OPTION** — focus on field deployment/service/data, not whole-robot manufacturing.

# 4. Cross-Border Compliance, Localization & Managed Overseas Operations

## Observable facts

A 2026 multi-agency Ministry of Commerce guidance supports cross-border ecommerce, overseas warehouses, seller/platform overseas expansion and **compliant overseas operations**. It specifically calls for cross-border ecommerce compliance-service platforms and stronger localization capabilities.

In February 2026, China's national overseas comprehensive service platform went live, integrating legal, tax, finance, logistics, customs, trade and other service resources. The Ministry of Commerce states that demand for professional overseas services has grown as more Chinese companies expand abroad.

Primary sources:

- MOFCOM ecommerce high-quality development guidance: https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zcfb/zhzc/art/2026/art_7e1dc12697b744fd89e6110673ac117c.html
- MOFCOM national overseas comprehensive service platform launch: https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/xwfb/rcxwfb/art/2026/art_ffe150f8dc2448af856814d881616df6.html

## Opportunity Radar inference

As “going global” becomes more common, value may move from generic market-entry advice toward **execution-heavy local services**:

- compliance coordination;
- tax/legal/resource routing;
- supplier/QC and overseas fulfillment;
- localization operations;
- country-specific marketplace operations;
- after-sales/customer support;
- recurring risk monitoring.

## Payer hypothesis

- exporter / brand owner;
- cross-border merchant;
- overseas operating entity;
- trade/market-entry budget.

## Best-fit career transitions

- export sales/operator;
- sourcing/logistics operator;
- cross-border ecommerce operator;
- trade compliance / localization specialist.

## Power Pool hypothesis

Generic information is weak because public support platforms can make information easier to access.

Stronger power may form in:

- trusted local execution;
- country/category specialization;
- recurring compliance workflow;
- supplier/customer network;
- performance data;
- integrated fulfillment and after-sales capability.

## Leading indicators

- more companies seeking specialized country-level service;
- recurring compliance/localization spend;
- professional service providers specializing by destination/industry;
- overseas operating teams growing.

## Confirmation indicators

- repeat contracts across multiple operating cycles;
- measurable reduction in compliance/logistics errors;
- customer expansion from one service into multiple recurring workflows;
- durable margin after local delivery costs.

## Kill criteria

- public/free information eliminates willingness to pay for the chosen layer;
- business remains one-off consulting;
- regulatory complexity creates liability that cannot be priced;
- working capital or FX risk dominates economics.

## Current stance

**BUY A REAL OPTION** — sell execution around one country/category bottleneck before building a broad platform.

# Portfolio View

These four opportunities share a pattern:

```text
new general capability / infrastructure
        ↓
headline attention goes to the core technology
        ↓
real-world deployment reveals last-mile bottlenecks
        ↓
career operators with domain proximity become more valuable
        ↓
service is the cheap entry wedge
        ↓
repeatable service can become data / software / product / network
```

That pattern is precisely what Opportunity Radar is designed to detect.
